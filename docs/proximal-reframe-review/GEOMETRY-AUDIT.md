# Independent proximal-geometry check

The refined FDI 36 lesion measures **2.414 × 1.484 mm** at the 5 µm displacement threshold; its maximum depression is **0.769 mm** in the model’s illustrative scale. The preceding release (`510a734`) measured 2.425 × 1.481 mm and 0.769 mm. The removed volume changes from 0.7831 to 0.9356 mm³: the new cavity distributes more of that depth over its footprint.

`refined_surface.py` reconstructs the healthy mesh independently of the browser code. Each midpoint refers only to earlier vertices. Original-vertex ancestry identifies the parent of every child triangle; the test checks that ancestry against the declared parent ID, child orientation, complete and nonoverlapping parent boundary intervals, paired internal edges and total parent area. Across all 611 replaced faces, the area-ratio error was zero and the largest child-point residual from its parent was 5.11 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. The other 54,573 source triangles remain untouched. The original attributes and the healthy global silhouette are preserved. Reconstructed P/N/T/UV and indexed topology also match the actual Three.js `refineCavitySurface` result byte for byte on both LODs.

The four wall widths come from perpendicular ray intersections with the actual healthy and indented surfaces on the same lesion-centered axes, with no material or normal-map effect. They describe these four profiles, not a clinical threshold of sharpness.

The refined tooth has one watertight component, consistent winding, Euler characteristic 2 and no seam crack. At five morph influences (0, ¼, ½, ¾ and 1), global nonadjacent self-intersections, inverted/degenerate triangles and unexpected changes outside the sparse set were zero. Across both LODs and all five poses, the 3,780 inter-tooth pair tests found no intersections. The 36 and 46 instances keep a **shared native source**, independently placed; only the left tooth is refined and morphed. The source GLBs, raw Dundee input, arch matrices and measured brush guide are unchanged relative to `510a734`.

`run-audit.mjs` constructs the real Three.js brush, expands every transformed instance, and samples the pre-alignment pose plus 41 insertion stations at both flexion extrema. The worker tests both LODs against the complete hard-surface assembly and the actual lifted, mature film faces returned by `createMatureFilm`. Bristles contacted the film on both teeth (desktop: 539 / 63 different triangles on 36 / 37; mobile: 383 / 32). No brush component crossed a hard tooth or the cavity floor. A conservative bound for these **83 tool poses per LOD** keeps all tested tool triangles at least 0.208 mm from the designated floor over all continuous linear morph influences. It projects the six healthy/mature end vertices of each floor triangle onto the plane normal to the wire; the resulting convex hull contains the intermediate triangle projections. This is not a general clinical safety claim.

`actualAuthoredCameraKeyframes` tests the actual desktop and compact camera builders and all 28 teeth. A sharp concavity can hide part of its own floor; the report gives measured fractions, not an assumption that every triangle must be visible. The deepest point and guide entrance remain visible in the cavity and result views, which expose over 99% of sampled floor area. The brush view emphasizes the entrance, with part of the floor behind 37. `capturedProductionCameras` replays the separately identified frozen numeric poses in [browser-camera-inputs.json](browser-camera-inputs.json); check its bundle/capture hashes before comparing a current screenshot. These are geometry-only rays to triangle centroids, not counts of displayed or clinically visible pixels.

The production review supplied 15 full-page PNGs (5 scenes × 3 viewports). The 9 arch-camera inputs used here are the 360×780, 390×844 and 1280×900 cavity, brush and result captures. All 9 camera-to-deepest-floor and camera-to-entry rays cleared the complete dentition. The captured cavity and result views each saw 1,265 of 1,267 floor-triangle centroids (~99.6% area), while the brush views saw roughly 80–82% of the floor area. This is the intentional distinction between photographing the cavity and looking along the working entrance. The reported floor box in CSS pixels is a pre-depth-test projection; the input also records the actual canvas CSS size, resting DPR and drawing-buffer dimensions.

The separate `smile-closure.py` test reads the current JS configuration and the same refined, mature lower surface. It tests all 196 upper/lower pairs for the full configured 5.8 mm vertical smile sweep on each LOD, not just its endpoint. There were no intersections. Increasing the travel to 6.4 mm, the positive control, detected a 21/31 collision on both LODs.

To reproduce from the repository root, install its Node dependencies and run `node docs/proximal-reframe-review/run-audit.mjs` with Blender 4.5 available through `BLENDER_BIN` or the workspace’s local tools directory. For the smile sweep run `blender --background --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python docs/proximal-reframe-review/smile-closure.py`. Both are CPU-only and do not modify source GLBs. `independent-geometry.json` and `smile-closure.json` contain the measured results and asset hashes. `brush-healthy.json` is a retained earlier healthy reference, not the mature-film result for this revision.
